ProjectedSphericalHarmonics.Disk — Type
Disk

Discretization of the unit disk, returned by disk(Mℓ, Mₘ). The grid is Mℓ + 1 Gauss–Legendre points in $s = \sqrt{1 - r^2}$ (clustered toward the rim) by 2Mₘ + 1 equispaced angles, stored as matrices of size (Mℓ + 1, 2Mₘ + 1). Fields used outside the package:

  • z (alias of ζ): the grid points as complex numbers $x + iy$; r, θ: polar coordinates
  • dz (alias of dζ): area weights, so sum(u .* D.dz) integrates u (see integral)
  • w: the weight $w = \sqrt{1 - |z|^2}$
  • Mℓ, Mₘ: maximum degree and order; Lspan ($0, …, Mℓ$) and Mspan ($0, …, Mₘ, -Mₘ, …, -1$) label the rows and columns of a coefficient array
  • even, odd: masks of the coefficients with $ℓ + m$ even or odd (and $|m| ≤ ℓ$)

The other fields are precomputed operator matrices in coefficient space.

ProjectedSphericalHarmonics.domain — Function
domain(γ, Mℓ, Mₘ; map=:auto)
domain(γ, M; map=:auto)

Discretization of the inside of the closed curve γ, the image of the unit disk under a map $z = F(ζ)$ with $F(e^{it}) = γ(t)$.

Arguments

  • γ : the curve, sampled at equispaced parameter values $t$, counterclockwise
  • Mℓ : maximum radial degree
  • Mₘ : maximum azimuthal order
  • map : the map from the disk
    • :auto (default): the harmonic map, or the Winslow map if the harmonic map folds (with a message)
    • :harmonic: the harmonic extension of the samples (see harmonicmap). The parametrization of γ places the grid: the boundary point $e^{it}$ of the disk goes to $γ(t)$. γ = a cos t + i b sin t gives the affine map of ellipse. One-to-one for convex curves; a map that folds is an error. No hypersingular operator 𝒩.
    • :winslow: the Winslow (elliptic grid generation) map, whose inverse is harmonic (see winslowmap). One-to-one for any smooth simply connected domain, so it handles non-convex shapes on which the harmonic map folds, at a higher cost to build (seconds at M = 16). The parametrization of γ places the grid, as for :harmonic. Strongly deformed shapes are an error. No hypersingular operator 𝒩.
    • :conformal: the conformal map with $F(0) = 0$ (see conformalmap), which ignores the parametrization. The origin must be inside the curve (not checked). It crowds the grid for elongated shapes: a 4:1 ellipse gives capacitance errors of about 40% at M = 32.

Returns

  • Domain containing discretization information. Ω.f is the map; Ω.df is $F′$ for the conformal map and $ζ ↦ (∂F, \bar∂F)$ for the harmonic and Winslow maps.
Experimental

Mapped domains are experimental: their accuracy and interface may change. The operators are dense (moderate M only), and suspensions of such bodies have no close evaluation. For ellipses, use ellipse.

ProjectedSphericalHarmonics.Domain — Type
Domain

Discretization of a non-circular domain, the image of the unit disk under a map $z = f(ζ)$: returned by ellipse(a, b, Mℓ, Mₘ) (an affine map) and domain(γ, Mℓ, Mₘ) (a harmonic or conformal map). It wraps the Disk of the reference grid (its fields, such as even and w, are reachable from the Domain) and adds the mapped points z, the area weights dz (with the Jacobian of the map), and the operators of the domain as matrices in coefficient space (K̂_S, K̂_N, K̂_G). Functions on a Domain are sampled at Ω.z; the weight Ω.w is the disk's, $\sqrt{1 - |ζ|^2}$, which vanishes on the boundary like the square root of the distance to it. For a domain, the operators on the grid (K_S, K_N, K_G) are formed when accessed, as dense matrices.

ProjectedSphericalHarmonics.psh — Function
psh(u, D; parity=:even)

PSH coefficients of the grid values u on D (a Disk or Domain), as a new array; psh! transforms in place. The coefficients have the shape of the grid: entry [ℓ + 1, j] is the coefficient of $y_ℓ^m$ with $m$ = D.Mspan[j]. Only the coefficients of the requested parity (:even, ℓ + m even, or :odd) are computed; the others are zero. Even PSH are polynomials in $x, y$; odd ones are $w$ times polynomials. u may also be a number (a constant) or a tuple of arrays (transformed componentwise). The transform is exact for functions in the span of the retained modes: psh(ipsh(û, D), D) == û to rounding.

Laplace operators

ProjectedSphericalHarmonics.𝒮 — Function
𝒮(u, Ω)

Single layer potential of the 3D Laplacian on the disk or a Domain Ω, evaluated on Ω,

\[\mathcal{S}[u](x) = \frac{1}{4\pi} \int_Ω \frac{u(y)}{|x - y|}\, dy.\]

u is a density of the form $g/w$ with $g$ smooth, the edge behavior of solutions of first-kind equations on an open surface; g = u .* w is expanded in even PSH. On the unit disk these are eigenfunctions, $\mathcal{S}[y_ℓ^m/w] = (λ_ℓ^m/4)\, y_ℓ^m$ (λlm), so 𝒮 is diagonal in coefficient space; on a Domain it is a sparse (ellipse) or dense (domain) matrix there. u may be a number (a constant density).

ProjectedSphericalHarmonics.𝒮⁻¹ — Function
𝒮⁻¹(f, Ω)

Inverse of 𝒮: the density $u$ with $\mathcal{S}[u] = f$ on Ω, for smooth f. The result has the $1/w$ edge singularity: on the unit disk 𝒮⁻¹(1, D) is $4/(π w)$. f may be a number.

ProjectedSphericalHarmonics.𝒩 — Function
𝒩(u, Ω)

Hypersingular operator of the 3D Laplacian on the disk or a Domain Ω (the normal derivative of the double layer potential, kernel $1/(4π|x - y|^3)$ in the finite-part sense). u is a density vanishing like $w$ at the edge ($w$ times a smooth function), expanded in odd PSH; on the unit disk these are eigenfunctions, $\mathcal{N}[y_ℓ^m] = -y_ℓ^m/(λ_ℓ^m w)$ for $ℓ + m$ odd. Returns a smooth function. u may be a number.

ProjectedSphericalHarmonics.𝒩⁻¹ — Function
𝒩⁻¹(f, Ω)

Inverse of 𝒩: the density $u$ (vanishing like $w$ at the edge) with $\mathcal{N}[u] = f$ on Ω, for smooth f. f may be a number.

Volume potentials

ProjectedSphericalHarmonics.𝒱 — Function
𝒱(u, D::Disk)

Volume potential of the 2D Laplacian on the unit disk,

\[\mathcal{V}[u](x) = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_D \log|x - y|\, u(y)\, dy,\]

so that $Δ\mathcal{V}[u] = u$ and 𝒱(1, D) $= (|x|^2 - 1)/4$. A dense direct sum over the grid with the singularity subtracted ($O(N^2)$ in the number of grid points N); accurate to about the quadrature error of a log singularity on the grid, not spectrally.

ProjectedSphericalHarmonics.ℬ — Function
ℬ(u, D::Disk; κ=0)

Volume potential of the 2D bilaplacian on the unit disk,

\[\mathcal{B}[u](x) = \frac{1}{8\pi} \int_D |x - y|^2 \left(\log|x - y| - 1 + κ\right) u(y)\, dy,\]

so that $Δ^2 \mathcal{B}[u] = u$; κ adds a multiple of the biharmonic $\int |x - y|^2 u$. A dense direct sum over the grid ($O(N^2)$), algebraically accurate (the kernel is continuous).

ProjectedSphericalHarmonics.𝒯 — Function
𝒯(u, D::Disk)

Single layer of the 3D bilaplacian on the unit disk,

\[\mathcal{T}[u](x) = \frac{1}{8\pi} \int_D |x - y|\, u(y)\, dy,\]

computed spectrally from four applications of 𝒮 ($|x - y| = |x - y|^2/|x - y|$). Like 𝒮, it expects a density with the $1/w$ edge behavior.

Stokes flow

ProjectedSphericalHarmonics.𝒮_st — Function
𝒮_st(f, Ω; μ_bottom=1.0, μ_top=0.0)

Single layer operator for in-plane Stokes flow driven by forces in the plane z = 0, which separates fluids of viscosity μ_bottom (below) and μ_top (above). The Green's function is

G(r) = (I + r̂ r̂ᵀ) / (4π (μ_bottom + μ_top) |r|).

The default (μ_bottom = 1, μ_top = 0) is a half-space of unit viscosity under a stress-free surface. Free space is μ_bottom = μ_top, giving the usual 1/(8π μ_bottom).

Arguments

  • f : array of force values, given as a tuple (fx, fy)
  • Ω : domain discretization
  • μ_bottom, μ_top : viscosities below and above the plane (default: 1.0, 0.0, a half-space under a stress-free surface; set both equal for free space)

Returns

  • array of velocity values, given as a tuple (ux, uy)
𝒮_st(i, j, f, Ω; μ_bottom=1.0, μ_top=0.0)

Single layer operator for in-plane Stokes flow (see 𝒮_st(f, Ω)), evaluated at a specific component

Arguments

  • i : row index of the Green's function matrix (1 for x-component, 2 for y-component)
  • j : column index of the Green's function matrix (1 for x-component, 2 for y-component)
  • f : array of force values, given as a tuple (fx, fy)
  • Ω : domain discretization
  • μ_bottom, μ_top : viscosities below and above the plane (default: 1.0, 0.0, a half-space under a stress-free surface; set both equal for free space)

Returns

  • array of function values for the specified component
𝒮_st(f::NTuple{3}, Ω; μ=1.0)

Stokes single layer of a force density with a normal component, f = (fx, fy, fz), in free space: one fluid of viscosity μ above and below the plane. Returns (ux, uy, uz). The in-plane and normal parts decouple on the plane: (ux, uy) is 𝒮_st((fx, fy), Ω; μ_bottom=μ, μ_top=μ), and

uz = 𝒮[fz] / (2μ)      (G_zz = 1/(8πμ|r|), the Laplace single layer `𝒮`).

The normal problem is not defined in a half-space or between two fluids (a normal force would deform the interface), so μ_bottom and μ_top are rejected here. Components may be numbers.

𝒮_st(tgt, f, Ω; μ_bottom=1.0, μ_top=0.0)

Evaluate the in-plane Stokes single layer potential at points outside the domain.

The Green's function is G(r) = (I + r̂ r̂ᵀ) / (4π (μbottom + μtop) |r|), where r = x - y is the 2D separation vector. Direct quadrature is used since tgt lies outside the support and there is no singularity.

Arguments

  • tgt : evaluation points as a complex array (x + iy)
  • f : force density as a tuple (fx, fy)
  • Ω : domain discretization
  • μ_bottom, μ_top : viscosities below and above the plane (default: 1.0, 0.0; see 𝒮_st(f, Ω))

Returns

  • Tuple (ux, uy) with the same shape as tgt

ProjectedSphericalHarmonics.stokes_mobility_solve — Function
stokes_mobility_solve(uinf, uslip, F, Ω; M=[], μ_bottom=1.0, μ_top=0.0)

Solve for the velocity and angular velocity of a domain Ω in in-plane Stokes flow given background flow, forces, and torques.

Arguments

  • uinf : background velocity field (tuple of x and y components)
  • uslip : prescribed slip velocity (tuple of x and y components), or nothing for no-slip
  • F : applied force and torque vector [Fx, Fy, T]
  • Ω : domain discretization (e.g., disk or ellipse)
  • M : precomputed mobility matrix (optional; must be computed with the same viscosities)
  • μ_bottom, μ_top : viscosities below and above the plane (default: 1.0, 0.0, a half-space under a stress-free surface; set both equal for free space)

Returns

  • Tuple (U, ω) where U is the velocity vector and ω is the angular velocity

Normal forces (free space)

With F = [Fx, Fy, Fz, Tx, Ty, Tz] (six components), the body also moves out of the plane, in free space with one fluid of viscosity μ (keyword, default 1.0; μ_bottom and μ_top are rejected). uinf and uslip are then tuples (ux, uy, uz), and the result is (Ux, Uy, Uz), (Ωx, Ωy, Ωz). The in-plane and normal problems decouple; M is the in-plane matrix (computed with μ_bottom = μ_top = μ) and Mn the normal one (stokes_normal_mobility_matrix), both computed here if not given.

ProjectedSphericalHarmonics.stokes_normal_mobility_matrix — Function
stokes_normal_mobility_matrix(Ω; μ=1.0)

The 3 × 3 matrix of the out-of-plane rigid motions of a body in free space (viscosity μ): entry [i, k] is the generalized force k ($F_z$, $T_x = \int y f_z$, $T_y = -\int x f_z$) of the motion i (translation $U_z$, rotations $Ω_x$, $Ω_y$, with $u_z = U_z + Ω_x y - Ω_y x$), the counterpart of stokes_mobility_matrix for the normal direction. For the unit disk it is diag(16, 32/3, 32/3) μ.

Suspensions

ProjectedSphericalHarmonics.suspension_mobility_solve — Function
suspension_mobility_solve(xcm, θcm, uinf, uslip, F, Ω; eps=1e-6, M=[], S_self=nothing, method=:ewald, μ_bottom=1.0, μ_top=0.0)

Solve the N-body Stokes mobility problem via GMRES.

The boundary condition is uinf + S·f = A·U + uslip, giving the linear system [S, -A; B, 0] [f; U] = [uslip - uinf; F]. For rigid particles with no prescribed slip, pass uslip = nothing (or a zero function). For squirmers, uslip is the prescribed surface slip velocity.

Both uinf and uslip can be functions z -> (ux, uy) evaluated at the actual body positions xcm[i] + exp(iθcm[i]) * Ω.z, or pre-evaluated vectors of N tuples.

Preconditioned by independent single-body mobility solves (block-diagonal).

Arguments

  • xcm : vector of N complex center positions
  • θcm : vector of N orientations (radians)

suspension_velocity_solve(xcm, θcm, u_body, Ω; eps=1e-6, method=:ewald, S_self=nothing, μ_bottom=1.0, μ_top=0.0)

ProjectedSphericalHarmonics.suspension_velocity_eval — Function
suspension_velocity_eval(xcm, θcm, f_body, Ω; eps=1e-6, close_eval=true, close_radius=CLOSE_RADIUS, method=:ewald, S_self=nothing, μ_bottom=1.0, μ_top=0.0)

Velocity at the collocation points of every body due to the force densities f_body of all of them: the forward map of suspension_velocity_solve. Body i is Ω rotated by θcm[i] and centered at xcm[i]; f_body[i] is a tuple (fx, fy) on Ω's grid in the body frame, and the result is a vector of such tuples of velocities, also in each body's frame. Self terms are spectral (𝒮_st); cross terms are one fast sum (method = :ewald or :fmm, tolerance eps), corrected by the closed form at targets near another disk or ellipse unless close_eval = false. See suspension_mobility_solve for the other keywords.

ProjectedSphericalHarmonics.suspension_self_matrix — Function
suspension_self_matrix(Ω)

Within-body block of the fast sum's node quadrature, in the body frame: the 2Nz × 2Nz matrix taking a body's node stoklets (fx·dz; fy·dz) to the velocities at its own nodes, i = j excluded, in the FMM3D normalization G/(8π). Every body shares Ω and the Stokeslet is rotation covariant (G(Rr) = R G(r) Rᵀ), so one matrix serves all bodies at any position and orientation. It depends on Ω only: build it once and pass it to the suspension solves as S_self to reuse it across time steps. Memory is 32 Nz² bytes (36 MiB at Nz = 1089).
