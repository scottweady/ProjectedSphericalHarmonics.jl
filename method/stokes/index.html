Stokes flow

In-plane Stokes flow

A thin body in the plane $z = 0$, between a fluid of viscosity $\mu_\mathrm{bottom}$ below and one of viscosity $\mu_\mathrm{top}$ above, moves in its plane. A force density $f$ on it drives a flow whose velocity in the plane is

\[u(x) = \mathcal{S}_\mathrm{st}[f](x) = \int_\Omega G(x - y)\, f(y)\, dy, \qquad G(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\mu}\left(\frac{\mathbf{I}}{|\mathbf{r}|} + \frac{\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}^T}{|\mathbf{r}|^3}\right), \qquad \mu = \mu_\mathrm{bottom} + \mu_\mathrm{top}.\]

This is the in-plane block of the 3D Stokeslet. It covers several settings:

  • the default, $\mu_\mathrm{bottom} = 1$ and $\mu_\mathrm{top} = 0$: a half-space of unit viscosity under a stress-free surface;
  • equal viscosities: free space, where $G$ is the usual $1/(8\pi\mu_\mathrm{bottom})$ Stokeslet;
  • unequal viscosities: an interface between two fluids.

A translating disk of unit radius has drag $16\mu/3$ in the half-space and $32\mu/3$ in free space, and the same values hold for the torque of a rotating disk.

Angular kernels

Write $x - y = |x - y|(\cos\phi, \sin\phi)$. Each entry of $G$ is a function of the angle $\phi$ alone, divided by $|x - y|$:

\[4\pi\mu\, G = \frac{1}{|x - y|}\begin{pmatrix} 1 + \cos^2\phi & \sin\phi\cos\phi \\ \sin\phi\cos\phi & 1 + \sin^2\phi \end{pmatrix}.\]

A kernel $g(\phi)/|x - y|$ with $g(\phi) = \sum_k \hat g_k e^{ik\phi}$ acts on the PSH by shifting the order: each Fourier mode $k$ sends $y_\ell^m/w$ to a multiple $C_{\ell}^{m,k}\, y_\ell^{m+k}$ (Clmn), at the same degree $\ell$. The matrix in coefficient space is therefore sparse, with one diagonal per Fourier mode of $g$. laplace3d_angular_matrix builds it from $g$ or its Fourier coefficients and keeps modes until they fall below a tolerance (1e-14 by default). $\cos^2\phi$, $\sin\phi\cos\phi$ and $\sin^2\phi$ have the modes $0, \pm 2$, so on the disk each block of $G$ has three diagonals. The ellipse's blocks have more, since its angular factor is a nonpolynomial function of $\phi$ (see Non-circular domains).

𝒮_st(f, Ω) and 𝒮_st⁻¹(u, Ω) act on tuples (fx, fy) with the $2 \times 2$ block of these sparse matrices (Ω.K̂_G), on the even coefficients of $w f$. As with 𝒮, the force density of a first-kind solve behaves like $1/w$ at the edge, and 𝒮_st⁻¹ returns it in that form. The inverse is a sparse LU solve of a matrix of size twice the number of even modes.

𝒮_st(tgt, f, Ω) evaluates the flow at points off the body by direct quadrature over the grid. It is accurate away from the body, and loses accuracy within a few grid spacings of it.

The mobility problem of one body

A rigid body moving with velocity $U$ and rotation rate $\omega$ in a background flow $u_\infty$, with an optional slip velocity $u_s$ on its surface, carries the force density $f$ that satisfies

\[\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{st}[f] + u_\infty = U + \omega\, (-y, x) + u_s \ \text{ on } \Omega, \qquad \int_\Omega f\, dA = (F_x, F_y), \quad \int_\Omega (x f_y - y f_x)\, dA = T,\]

with the applied force and torque given. Let $e_1 = (1, 0)$, $e_2 = (0, 1)$ and $e_3 = (-y, x)$ be the rigid motions. The force and torque of $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{st}^{-1} e_k$ form the $3 \times 3$ resistance matrix, which stokes_mobility_matrix(Ω) returns ($M$ below). By linearity, stokes_mobility_solve(uinf, uslip, F, Ω):

  1. computes the force density $\mathcal{S}_\mathrm{st}^{-1}[u_\infty - u_s]$ and its force and torque $F_\infty$;
  2. solves $M\,(U, \omega) = F + F_\infty$.

Each call costs one inverse, or four if M is not passed.

Normal forces

On the plane $z = 0$ the separation $x - y$ has no $z$ component, so the 3D Stokeslet's entries $G_{xz}$ and $G_{yz}$ vanish there. A normal force density $f_z$ therefore drives only the normal velocity, and an in-plane force only in-plane velocity. In free space, with one fluid of viscosity $\mu_f$ above and below,

\[u_z(x) = \frac{1}{8\pi\mu_f} \int_\Omega \frac{f_z(y)}{|x - y|}\, dy = \frac{1}{2\mu_f}\, \mathcal{S}[f_z](x),\]

the Laplace single layer, so $f_z = 2\mu_f\, \mathcal{S}^{-1}[u_z]$ has the same $1/w$ edge behavior and the same spectral accuracy, on every domain. 𝒮_st and 𝒮_st⁻¹ take 3-tuples for this, with the keyword μ ($= \mu_f$). Their in-plane part is the two-component operator with μ_bottom = μ_top = μ.

A rigid body's out-of-plane motions are a translation $U_z$ and two tilts, $u_z = U_z + \Omega_x y - \Omega_y x$, with the force $F_z = \int f_z$ and the torques $T_x = \int y f_z$ and $T_y = -\int x f_z$. Their $3 \times 3$ matrix is stokes_normal_mobility_matrix(Ω; μ). For the unit disk it is $\mathrm{diag}(16, 32/3, 32/3)\,\mu_f$, the classical broadside drag and tilting torque, which the tests check. With six-component forces $[F_x, F_y, F_z, T_x, T_y, T_z]$ and a 3-tuple background flow, stokes_mobility_solve solves the in-plane and normal problems separately and returns $(U_x, U_y, U_z)$ and $(\Omega_x, \Omega_y, \Omega_z)$.

The decoupling needs the same fluid on both sides. In a half-space under a free surface, or at an interface between two fluids, the plane $z = 0$ is the interface itself, and a normal force would deform it. So the normal problem is free space only: these calls take μ and reject μ_bottom and μ_top.
