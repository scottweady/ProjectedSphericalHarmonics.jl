Limits

What the method does not do, and where it needs care.

Geometry

  • Surfaces are flat: a disk, an ellipse, or the inside of one smooth closed curve, in the plane $z = 0$. There are no holes, no corners, and no curved surfaces.
  • Mapped domains (domain) are experimental; the next items are their known limits.
  • The harmonic map is one-to-one for convex curves. A non-convex curve may make it fold; domain then uses the Winslow map, which does not fold but costs 5–10 times more to build (map = :harmonic makes a fold an ArgumentError instead). Harmonic and Winslow domains have no hypersingular operator 𝒩.
  • How deformed a mapped domain can be is limited by the spectral discretization: the operators need $|\mu| < 1$ between every pair of grid points (an ArgumentError otherwise) and converge more slowly as it approaches 1. Five-armed stars with valleys at half the arm length, or chiral pinwheels like the one in Mapped domains, reach six digits at $M = 32$; sharper arms and deeper valleys do not. The Winslow map costs seconds to build at $M = 16$ and about 30 s at $M = 32$.
  • The conformal map (map = :conformal) needs the origin inside the curve, and does not check. A curve that does not enclose it gives wrong operators without an error (see Mapped domains). It crowds the grid on elongated shapes: a 4:1 ellipse is off by 37% at $M = 32$.
  • The curve must be smooth and resolved by its samples.
  • domain builds dense matrices in coefficient space, of size about $N/4$ ($N$ grid points): 10 MiB at $M = 24$, growing like $M^4$ in memory and $M^5$ (conformal) or $M^6$ (harmonic) in time. Ellipses should use ellipse, which is sparse and cheap.

Operators

  • ℬ is a dense direct sum, $O(N^2)$, and converges algebraically. 𝒱 and 𝒯_vol are spectral only for smooth densities; 𝒯_vol at off-grid targets costs a polar quadrature per distinct $|x|$.
  • The differential operators (∂ζ, ∂ζ̄, grad, ∂x, ∂y, div, lap) divide by $w$ in coefficient space, which is ill-conditioned. They lose digits as the resolution grows. ∂n does not have this problem.
  • These are defined on the disk only: 𝒱, ℬ, 𝒯, 𝒯_vol, 𝒮𝒩⁻¹, Δ⁻¹, the differential operators, and the shape derivatives. The layer potentials 𝒮, 𝒩, their inverses, and the Stokes operators work on every domain.
  • Densities must have the edge behavior the operator expects: $g/w$ for 𝒮, 𝒯 and 𝒮_st, $w g$ for 𝒩. A density without it, such as a smooth density passed to 𝒮, still converges, but only algebraically.
  • 𝒮_st(tgt, f, Ω) (the flow off a body) is direct quadrature over the grid, inaccurate within a few grid spacings of the body.

Normal forces

  • The normal (out-of-plane) problem is defined in free space only (μ, one fluid above and below). It is available for single bodies; the suspension solver is in-plane only.

Suspensions

  • Every body is a copy of the same Ω. Bodies of different shapes or sizes need separate discretizations, which the solver does not support.
  • Bodies must not overlap. For disks and ellipses, the close-evaluation setup throws if they do; bodies from domain are not checked.
  • Close evaluation applies to disks and ellipses only. Bodies from domain use the node sum at every target, which loses accuracy when they come within a few grid spacings of each other.
  • The close-evaluation zone close_radius is fixed ($c = 8$ grid spacings by default) and does not follow eps. At loose eps it corrects more targets than needed. At tight eps and low $M$ it corrects too few: for two disks at $M = 16$, the cross velocity levels off at 2e-10, set by the targets just outside the zone, and close_radius = 20 brings it to 3e-12 (see Validation).
  • The Ewald sum is tested against FMM3D at eps 1e-6 and 1e-10 (relative differences 1.7e-9 and 4.8e-10 on 12 disks), not at tighter tolerances.
  • The fast sums run on all threads, with their own OpenMP threads alongside Julia's. A heavily loaded machine, or a multithreaded BLAS spinning between steps, slows them noticeably.
