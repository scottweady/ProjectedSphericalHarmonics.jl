Limits

What the method does not do, and where it needs care.

Geometry

  • Surfaces are flat: a disk, an ellipse, or the inside of one smooth closed curve, in the plane $z = 0$. There are no holes, no corners, and no curved surfaces.
  • Conformally mapped domains (domain) are experimental; the next three items are their known limits.
  • domain(γ, M) needs the origin inside the curve, and does not check. A curve that does not enclose it gives wrong operators without an error (see Conformally mapped domains). The curve must be smooth and resolved by its samples.
  • domain converges more slowly as the shape gets more elongated, at a rate set by the conformal map, and more boundary samples do not help. For an ellipse with $a/b = 2$ built by domain, the conductor density is still off by 3e-2 at $M = 32$, where $a/b = 1.5$ reaches 6e-5 (see Validation).
  • domain builds dense $N \times N$ matrices ($N$ grid points): 123 MiB and 1.8 s at $M = 24$, growing like $M^4$ in memory. Ellipses should use ellipse, which is sparse and cheap.

Operators

  • The volume potentials 𝒱 and ℬ are dense direct sums, $O(N^2)$, and converge algebraically.
  • The differential operators (∂ζ, ∂ζ̄, grad, ∂x, ∂y, div, lap) divide by $w$ in coefficient space, which is ill-conditioned. They lose digits as the resolution grows. ∂n does not have this problem.
  • These are defined on the disk only: 𝒱, ℬ, 𝒯, 𝒮𝒩⁻¹, Δ⁻¹, the differential operators, and the shape derivatives. The layer potentials 𝒮, 𝒩, their inverses, and the Stokes operators work on every domain.
  • Densities must have the edge behavior the operator expects: $g/w$ for 𝒮, 𝒯 and 𝒮_st, $w g$ for 𝒩. A density without it, such as a smooth density passed to 𝒮, still converges, but only algebraically.
  • 𝒮_st(tgt, f, Ω) (the flow off a body) is direct quadrature over the grid, inaccurate within a few grid spacings of the body.

Suspensions

  • Every body is a copy of the same Ω. Bodies of different shapes or sizes need separate discretizations, which the solver does not support.
  • Bodies must not overlap. For disks and ellipses, the close-evaluation setup throws if they do; bodies from domain are not checked.
  • Close evaluation applies to disks and ellipses only. Bodies from domain use the node sum at every target, which loses accuracy when they come within a few grid spacings of each other.
  • The close-evaluation zone close_radius is fixed ($c = 8$ grid spacings by default) and does not follow eps. At loose eps it corrects more targets than needed. At tight eps and low $M$ it corrects too few: for two disks at $M = 16$, the cross velocity levels off at 2e-10, set by the targets just outside the zone, and close_radius = 20 brings it to 3e-12 (see Validation).
  • The Ewald sum is tested against FMM3D at eps 1e-6 and 1e-10 (relative differences 1.7e-9 and 4.8e-10 on 12 disks), not at tighter tolerances.
  • The fast sums run on all threads, with their own OpenMP threads alongside Julia's. A heavily loaded machine, or a multithreaded BLAS spinning between steps, slows them noticeably.
