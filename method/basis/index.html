The PSH basis

Projected spherical harmonics

A point $(x, y) = (r\cos\theta, r\sin\theta)$ of the unit disk is the projection of the point $(x, y, \sqrt{1 - r^2})$ of the upper unit hemisphere. The projected spherical harmonics are the spherical harmonics at that point:

\[y_\ell^m(r, \theta) = N_\ell^m\, P_\ell^{|m|}(w)\, e^{im\theta}, \qquad w = \sqrt{1 - r^2}, \qquad N_\ell^m = \sqrt{\frac{2\ell + 1}{2\pi}\, \frac{(\ell - |m|)!}{(\ell + |m|)!}},\]

for $\ell \ge 0$ and $|m| \le \ell$ (ylm(ℓ, m, z), with Nlm(ℓ, m) the normalization). The hemisphere's area element is $dA/w$, so harmonics of the same parity of $\ell + m$ are orthonormal in the weighted inner product

\[\langle u, v \rangle = \int_D \frac{u(x)\, \overline{v(x)}}{\sqrt{1 - |x|^2}}\, dx.\]

(Each parity is the restriction to the hemisphere of the harmonics that are even, or odd, under reflection through the equator, so each is complete on the hemisphere by itself.)

Parity and the edge. $P_\ell^{|m|}(w)$ is $(1 - w^2)^{|m|/2} = r^{|m|}$ times a polynomial in $w$ of degree $\ell - |m|$, which has only even powers when $\ell + m$ is even and only odd powers otherwise. Since $w^2 = 1 - r^2$:

  • Even $y_\ell^m$ ($\ell + m$ even) are polynomials in $x$ and $y$ of degree $\ell$.
  • Odd $y_\ell^m$ are $w$ times polynomials of degree $\ell - 1$, so they vanish on the rim like $w$.

A smooth function is an even expansion. A function that vanishes like $w$ (a double-layer density) is an odd expansion. A function singular like $1/w$ (a single-layer density) is $1/w$ times an even expansion. Each of these converges as fast as the smooth part allows.

The grid

disk(Mℓ, Mₘ) keeps the modes $\ell \le M_\ell$, $|m| \le M_m$. Its grid is the tensor product of

  • $M_\ell + 1$ Gauss–Legendre points in $s = \sqrt{1 - r^2} \in (0, 1)$, so radii $r = \sqrt{1 - s^2}$ cluster toward the rim, and
  • $2M_m + 1$ equispaced angles $\theta$.

Functions are matrices of values on this grid, (Mℓ + 1) × (2Mₘ + 1), and the coefficient arrays have the same shape: row $\ell + 1$, and one column per order $m$, in the order $0, 1, \dots, M_m, -M_m, \dots, -1$ (D.Mspan). The masks D.even and D.odd mark the entries of each parity with $|m| \le \ell$.

Since $r\,dr/w = -ds$, the weighted inner product is $\int_0^1 \int_0^{2\pi} u \bar v\, d\theta\, ds$, which the grid integrates exactly for products of modes in the retained range. The area weights D.dz ($r\,dr\,d\theta = w\, ds\, d\theta$) integrate smooth functions to the same order, and integrate $g/w$ with $g$ smooth as accurately as $g$.

Transforms

psh(u, D; parity) computes the coefficients of one parity, and ipsh(û, D; parity) evaluates an expansion of one parity on the grid. Each is an FFT in $\theta$, followed, for each order $m$, by a sum over $\ell$ in which $y_\ell^m$ at the grid radii comes from the three-term recurrence of the associated Legendre functions in $\ell$ (coefficients precomputed in D.a, D.am1). The cost is $O(M_\ell^2 M_m)$ for the sums plus $O(M_\ell M_m \log M_m)$ for the FFTs. A transform pair is exact for expansions in the retained modes: psh(ipsh(û, D), D) returns û to rounding. Other routines built on the transforms:

  • upsample(u, D, Nr, Nθ) evaluates the expansion of u on a finer grid.
  • trace(u, D) evaluates it on the rim.
  • psh_matrix and ipsh_matrix build the dense matrices of the transforms, entry by entry from the radial basis and the Fourier factors.
  • integral(u, Ω) is sum(u .* Ω.dz).

Differentiation

With $\partial_\zeta = (\partial_x - i\,\partial_y)/2$, $w\,\partial_\zeta y_\ell^m$ is a multiple of the mode $y_\ell^{m-1}$ (∂ylm∂ζ), which has the other parity. So ∂ζ of an even expansion is computed in two steps in coefficient space: shift each coefficient to order $m - 1$ with its closed-form factor, which gives $w\, \partial_\zeta u$ as an odd expansion, then remove the factor $w$ by solving with the matrix of multiplication by $w$. That solve is ill-conditioned, so ∂ζ, ∂ζ̄, grad, ∂x, ∂y, div and lap lose digits as the resolution grows. They are for smooth functions at moderate resolution, and their docstrings carry a warning. The normal derivative on the rim, ∂n, is a weighted sum of the even coefficients with closed-form weights and does not have this problem.
