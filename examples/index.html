Examples

The scripts in examples/ check the package against exact solutions. They have their own environment: from the package root, run julia --project=examples -e 'using Pkg; Pkg.instantiate()' once, then julia --project=examples examples/<name>.jl. The outputs below are from a laptop, each script taking 8–20 s, most of it compilation.

Eigenfunctions of the single layer (eigenfunctions.jl)

Applies 𝒮 to $y_5^3/w$ and 𝒮⁻¹ to $y_5^3$ on disk(64, 16), and compares with the eigenvalue relation $\mathcal{S}[y_\ell^m/w] = (\lambda_\ell^m/4)\, y_\ell^m$:

Max error in 𝒮 for (l,m) = (5,3): 4.4e-16
Max error in 𝒮⁻¹ for (l,m) = (5,3): 4.2e-9

The inverse's error is that of $y/w$ near the rim, where $w$ is small: dividing by $w$ amplifies rounding.

The Poisson problem (poisson.jl)

Solves $\Delta u = -1$ in the disk with $u = 0$ on the rim, by Δ⁻¹(-1, 0, D) on disk(64, 16), against $u = (1 - r^2)/4$:

Max error in Δ⁻¹ for f = -1, g = 0: 6.0e-15

Drag and torque on a disk (stokes.jl)

The force on a disk translating at unit speed, and the torque on one rotating at unit rate, under a stress-free surface (the default viscosities), on disk(128, 64). Both are $16/3$ exactly:

force on a translating disk: 5.333333333336814
torque on a rotating disk: 5.333333333338558

An ellipse, two ways (ellipse.jl)

The charge density of an ellipse at unit potential, and the drag on an ellipse translating along its major axis, for $a = 1.5$, $b = 1$, at $M = 32$. The ellipse is discretized twice: by ellipse (the affine map) and by domain from 127 samples of its boundary (the conformal map). The references are the closed forms $\sigma\sqrt{1 - x^2/a^2 - y^2/b^2} = 2/(b\,K(1 - b^2/a^2))$ and the drag $4\pi/A$ with $A$ from complete elliptic integrals:

Using angle discretization
Error in single layer potential: 8.8e-11
Error in drag force on an ellipse: 9.4e-14
Using conformal discretization
Error in single layer potential: 6.6e-5
Error in drag force on an ellipse: 6.1e-9

The affine map is exact, so ellipse is limited only by the expansion. The conformal map is computed numerically from the boundary samples, which limits domain. The pointwise density, measured against the $1/w$ form, is the most sensitive to the map's accuracy near the boundary.

A second-kind equation by GMRES (concentration.jl)

Solves $\sigma + 2\beta\, \mathcal{S}[\sigma] = 2\beta$ in coefficient space with the package's gmres wrapper, for $\beta = 0.1, 1, 10, 100$ on disk(128, 64). As $\beta \to \infty$ the solution tends to that of $\mathcal{S}[\sigma] = 1$, $\sigma_0 = 4/(\pi w)$. The script prints the difference at the center:

β = 0.1,  |σ(0) - σ₀(0)| = 1.09    (GMRES: 3 iterations)
β = 1,    |σ(0) - σ₀(0)| = 0.314   (5 iterations)
β = 10,   |σ(0) - σ₀(0)| = 0.0879  (8 iterations)
β = 100,  |σ(0) - σ₀(0)| = 0.0218  (17 iterations)

The difference falls roughly like $1/\beta$, while the iteration count grows with $\beta$, as the operator approaches the first-kind $\mathcal{S}$.

Stability of a growing droplet (microbial_droplet.jl)

A linear stability calculation that combines most of the Laplace operators. A droplet grows from a uniform concentration with buoyant forcing ($\mathrm{Ra} = \beta = 1$). Its base state is built from:

  • the volume potentials 𝒱 and ℬ;
  • 𝒩⁻¹ for the pressure;
  • 𝒮 for the stream function;
  • ∂n for the normal velocity of the rim.

The perturbation for each azimuthal mode $m$ comes from the shape derivatives δ𝒩, δ𝒮, δ𝒱 and δℬ. On disk(32) the script prints the growth rate $\sigma_m$ for $m = 0, \dots, 8$. For $m \ge 2$ these agree to 2–5e-6 with the closed form $\sigma_b = \frac{\beta\,\mathrm{Ra}}{96}\left(\frac{m\,\lambda_1^0\lambda_m^m}{4} - \frac{9}{2(m - 1)(2m + 1)}\right)$, which the script computes but does not print ($-0.0015625$ at $m = 2$, $0.0064174$ at $m = 3$). The rates for $m = 0$ and $1$, about 1e-6, are zero up to the same error.

(m, σₘ) = (2, -0.001560448350202179)
(m, σₘ) = (3, 0.006420022296074067)
(m, σₘ) = (4, 0.009660193515409412)
…
